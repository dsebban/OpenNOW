## P1

### Predeclared ratio is biased toward "fence dominant" by construction

- **Location:** `FfmpegDecoder::drain` (each attempt starts at `receive_frame`), together with `FfmpegDecoder::decode` (not shown). Also the T7 contract rule `SUM(fenceWait)/SUM(receive+conversion) > 0.5`.

- **Problem:**
  - `conversion` always contains `fenceWait`; the `wait > conversion` guard enforces this.
  - The denominator leaves out `avcodec_send_packet`. libavcodec decodes eagerly inside send_packet when its buffer frame is empty, so slice parsing and Vulkan decode recording/submission happen there. `receive_frame` mostly just hands over the buffered frame.
  - So `receive+conversion` is roughly fence wait plus a little copy setup. The ratio exceeds 0.5 whenever the wait is larger than that small overhead.
  - Illustrative case: a 2 ms wait plus 5.5 ms in send_packet scores about 0.83 ("flag"), while the wait's real share of the call is about 0.27.
  - The window therefore cannot conclude "fence not dominant versus codec", which is the decision it exists to make.

- **Evidence:**
  - The `wait > conversion` guard is in the diff.
  - Eager decoding in send_packet is inferred from libavcodec.
  - The `decode()` body is outside the shown ranges. Confirm that it is `send_packet` followed by `drain(false)`.

- **Fix:** no extra clock reads per successful single-output call.
  1. In `decode`, and optionally `flush`, when `frame_timing.is_some()`, take `Instant::now()` just before `send_packet` / `send_eof`.
  2. Pass that instant into `drain`.
  3. Build the call's first attempt with a new `Probe::begin_at(start)` instead of `begin()`, keeping the same `len == 0` UTC rule.
  4. Rename the stage `receive` → `sendReceive` and bump to `schema=2`.
  5. Update the one `receiveSumNs` test assertion and the ledger formula.
  6. Re-run the focused probe tests and the ignored P010 GPU test. No re-review is needed.

  The fallback, removing the >0.5 routing rule and reporting the ratio descriptively, leaves the window unable to choose the next move.

## P1 — Correct the projected UTC start after a format reset

- **Location:** `/Users/danielsivan/dev/OpenNOW/native/opennow-streamer/crates/opennow-streamer-platform-linux/src/timing.rs`, `vulkan_frame::Probe::record`, specifically the `attempt.utc_start_ns.or_else(...)` fallback.
- **Problem:** A format change during a partially accumulated batch can give the replacement batch a UTC start **later than its first measured frame actually started**. A batch overlapping the beginning of the steady observation interval could consequently appear wholly inside it, violating the predeclared inclusion rule.
- **Evidence:** `Probe::begin` captures UTC only when `len == 0`. On a mid-batch format change, `record` clears `len`, then reconstructs the first frame’s start using:
  
  ```rust
  utc_ns()?.checked_sub(ns(converted.saturating_duration_since(attempt.start)))
  ```
  
  The fresh UTC read happens **after** `converted`. Even with a stable wall clock, the reconstructed start is shifted forward by the intervening bookkeeping or scheduling delay. This follows directly from the timestamp boundaries; it is not a claimed runtime observation. The format-reset test supplies `utc_start_ns: Some(1)` on every attempt, so it bypasses this production fallback.
- **Fix:** In that fallback, read UTC followed immediately by a fresh `Instant`, then subtract the elapsed time from `attempt.start` to **that fresh Instant**. This accounts for the intervening delay and gives a conservative lower bound. Add a deterministic test exercising a format reset with no captured attempt UTC and delayed recording. Account for the additional clock read on this reset path; ordinary successful-frame timing remains unchanged.
